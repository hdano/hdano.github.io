You learned in music theory about Chord, that is a group of different notes played simultaneously (or sequentially). In singing, when two or more singers sing together with different notes and still sound good, it is called Harmony.

Here are the steps to get the harmony of a song:

Step 1: Find the melody

You need to know the syllables used in the melody of the song that you want to harmonize. This will be the basis for all other parts of the harmony.

Get the most comfortable key and start the “Do” syllable at the beginning of the scale.

For example, let's find the melody of the chorus (first line) of the song “Lord, I Offer My Life” by Don Moen:

Lyrics: Lord I of-fer my life to You
Syllables: Mi Mi Mi Fa Sol Sol Do Do

Step 2: Find the Higher and Lower Parts

As a basic rule, the higher and lower part should be one syllable (or scale note) away from the melody. Meaning, if the first syllable of the melody in our example is Mi, then the higher part will be Sol and the lower part will be Do.

  • Do - Higher part
  • Re
  • Mi - Melody
  • Fa
  • Sol - Lower part
  • La
  • Ti

The same goes with the rest of the syllables of the melody.

Lyrics: Lord I of- fer my life to You
Higher: Sol Sol Sol La Ti Ti Mi Mi
Melody: Mi Mi Mi Fa Sol Sol Do Do
Lower: Do Do Do Re Mi Mi La(low) La(low)

Step 3: Assign the Parts

Decide who will sing the melody and who will sing the other parts of the harmony.

There are three common ways to do this depending on song key:

  1. Soprano – Melody
    Alto – Lower part
    Tenor – Higher part in lower octave
    Bass - Melody
  2. Soprano – Higher part
    Alto – Melody
    Tenor – Lower part
    Bass - Higher part in lower octave
  3. Soprano – Lower part in higher octave
    Alto – Higher part
    Tenor – Melody
    Bass - Lower part
