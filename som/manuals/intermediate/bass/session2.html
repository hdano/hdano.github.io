If you are already playing bass guitar just by copying the bassline in songs and have no knowledge about why those notes are played together that way, here is a list of benefits of learning about bass scales to get you started:

  • It will help you understand that relationships between notes. This means some notes belong to particular note groups called scales and inside those groups, each note has its own function and character.
  • It will help you understand chord (in bass, note) progressions used in a song, that they are not just random notes selected by the song composer.
  • It will help you transpose the song to a higher or lower key. Worship leaders will not always take the original keys and most of the time change the key to the range they can sing.
  • It will help you improvise your own bass line because you already know which notes sound and feels right together.
  • It will help you write your own songs.

In music theory, we learned about the formula on getting the notes in major and minor scales. In bass guitar, we can find those notes and play the scales right away. But before that, here are a number of different ways in which you can know a scale or any other musical pattern. The more ways in which you know and understand a scale, the better off you will be.

By Sound

Learning the sound a pattern makes is the most important thing to pick up. After all, in music the sound is the final result we are seeking.

The more you play and practice, the more the sounds of music start popping out at you. You have to listen to what you play and practice in order to absorb it. Playing bass is not just about wiggling your fingers. It's about making sound. Training your ear will be a big part of your musical development.

By Interval Construction

Each scale has its own unique pattern of intervals. This is really the key to each scale’s sound. A scale’s intervallic makeup is what makes it sound the way it does. The better you know intervals, the easier it will be to learn scales and other patterns.

In music theory, we call this scales formula. We will talk about this on the next topic.

By Note Name

One important way of knowing a scale is by knowing the note names of the notes within the scale. For example, you should learn that the C major scale contains the notes C, D, E, F, G, A, and B.

Why would you want to know all the note names of a scale?! There are many reasons. The main reason is it will help you find your way around the fretboard a lot faster. It will make reading music easier. And, it will make improvising easier.

By Finger Patterns

When you learn a scale you need to be able to physically play it and locate it on the fretboard. All scales and note patterns form patterns on the bass fretboard.

One advantage of the bass is every pattern is movable up and down the fretboard and on other strings. This makes the physical motions of playing each scale or pattern easier to master and remember.

You have learned in music theory that the formula for the Major scale is:
T-T-S-T-T-T-S and the formula for the Minor scale is:

Things to notice in these scale examples:

  • Notice that we use multiple strings to achieve this. That is because we want to be able to play the scale in one area of the fretboard rather than moving your hands across the bass neck just to play a simple scale. This will make your hands and fingers relax while playing bass.
  • This scale starts at the second string (A) because that is where the lowest C note is closest to the nut. You can also play the lowest C note on the first string starting on the 8th fret and then use the same pattern.
  • The major and minor scales have pattern in fretboard that you can use in any key. Just find and start with the root note.
