The drummer is the timekeeper of the band. When the drummer is out of timing, the band suffers. So it is important for a drummer to improve his sense of timing. And the best way to keep track of your timing while playing is by listening to a metronome.

Below are some of the basic guides on using the metronome:

  1. Start your metronome on middle tempo of 80 BPM.
  2. Do not play the drums yet. Allow the sound of the metronome to sink in you. Let your mind follow those ticks first. Locate the accented tick which is the first note of the drum beat you are about to play.
  3. Start your drum beat at the accented tick. Make sure that you that all of your notes are in timings with the metronome ticks.
  4. Loop through the drum beat for 60 seconds straight. (If you made a mistake within 60 seconds, stop the metronome and relax your arms for a few seconds. Then start the metronome again and go back to Step 2.)
  5. After 60 seconds, stop the metronome and relax your arm for a few seconds.
  6. Set the metronome to another BPM as follows - 70, 65, 60, 65, 70, 80, 95, 110, 125, 140, 125, 110, 95, and 80 - in order and go back to Step 2

The drummer can break away from the drum beat by performing rhythms around the drum kit known as "drum fills". These drum fills are usually used:

  1. To signify a new section of a song
  2. To inject excitement or put color into a piece
  3. To punctuate a rhythm/melody that has some significance within the song.

Drum Fills Exercises

Instructions:

  1. Play the first 3 bars named “Basic Rock Beats”
  2. Then pick a fill to play as the fourth bar. Play one fill at least twice then pick another one.

Fill numbers 1 to 3 are called eight-note fills because they use a lot of eight notes. Fill numbers 4 to 9 are called sixteen-note fills.

Try perfecting the eight-note fills first before proceeding to the rest of the examples.

Use 120 BPM as a start but you change the tempos to faster or slower BPMs.
